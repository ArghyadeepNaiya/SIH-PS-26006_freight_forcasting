Sign in to your port

Each port has one password. It was generated for you by the project team and it is written in the file passwords dot t x t, which never leaves the team's machine and is never committed to version control. Signing in gives you access to your own port and to no other.

Seven East Coast ports are modelled in this system.

Three maritime words and four digits, joined by hyphens. For example, winch hyphen yardarm hyphen anchor hyphen eight seven two five. Passwords are built from words rather than random characters so that they can be read aloud and typed back accurately.

Your port at a glance

    Weather at your quay and your approach anchorage

    Forecast days are compared against the operating limits in this system's weather thresholds file. A day that breaches a limit is counted as a day of lost handling, and lost days are priced as demurrage in the landed cost that charterers see for your port. The approach anchorage is sampled roughly thirty five kilometres seaward, because weather there decides whether a vessel can reach you at all.

    Declare your handling areas

    List each laydown or handling area you can offer, how large it is, when it is free, and which classes of ship it can take. A charterer whose vessel class no area of yours accepts, or whose arrival window no free area covers, is told so by name and by date rather than being quoted a berth you cannot give them. An area with no dates is treated as open, because saying nothing about dates is not the same as saying the area is blocked.

    Rank the cargo your port most wants

    Rank one means the cargo you are shortest of and most want landed. This is intelligence for the buyer, and it is reported beside the ranking on the business dashboard, never inside it. Wanting a cargo does not make that cargo cheaper to ship to you, so this figure is never allowed to change a landed cost. What it does is tell a charterer where their cargo will find a buyer.

    Correct our operating figures for your port

    Almost every operating number this system holds about your port is an unverified assumption, and it says so on screen. You can replace them. Anything you enter here is labelled operator declared, dated, and attributed to you wherever a charterer sees it. Leave a field empty to keep our published or assumed figure.

    Port level operating figures

    How many tonnes of dry bulk your terminal actually moves off a ship in a working day.

    This is the single most expensive number on the page. Waiting time becomes demurrage, and demurrage lands directly in the cost per tonne that a charterer compares your port against its neighbours on.

    Leave empty if your port does not lighter at anchorage.
